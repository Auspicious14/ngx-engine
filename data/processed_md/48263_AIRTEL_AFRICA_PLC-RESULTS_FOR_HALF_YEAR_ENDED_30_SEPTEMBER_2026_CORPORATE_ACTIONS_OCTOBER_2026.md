Airtel Africa plc
Results for half year ended 30 September 2026
30 October 2026
Delivering sustainable and profitable growth while investing for the future
Operating highlights
• Continued investment in our network and digital capabilities is supporting increased customer adoption across all
segments. Our total customer base continued to grow at 11.6%, reaching 193.9 million customers, with a 15% growth
in our data customer base to 89.9 million. Demand for mobile money services remains strong, with mobile money
customers increasing by 22.4% to 60.9 million.
• The increase in smartphone penetration to 51.6% continued to drive the demand for data services, resulting in higher
smartphone data usage per customer of 13.3 GB, up from 10.1 GB in the prior period. The increase in data usage per
customer, together with continued improvements in the customer experience, drove constant currency1 data ARPU
growth of 10.7%, demonstrating our ability to effectively monetise growing data demand.
• The structural drivers of financial inclusion across Africa continue to support strong demand for mobile money services,
underpinned by enhanced distribution, accessible digital products and increased customer engagement. Continued
ecosystem expansion and digital adoption drove annualised total processed value (TPV) growth of 36.5% to $275bn in
Q2’27.
Financial performance
• Reported currency revenues reached $3,823m in H1’27, an increase of 28.2%, while in constant currency revenue grew
by 20.8%. The sustained growth across all our regions and business segments reflects the structural growth opportunity
and further evidence of our continued execution against this opportunity. The mobile services business continued to
grow at almost 19% in constant currency as data revenue growth of 28% continues to underpin growth. Mobile money
revenue growth in constant currency remained stable at 25.8%.
• Continued execution against our strategic priorities, alongside sustained investment, drove strong trends across all our
regions. In Francophone Africa, constant currency revenue growth accelerated to 20.3% in Q2’27, with Nigeria growing
by 24.4% and East Africa by 18.6%.
• Despite increased inflationary pressure from higher fuel costs, underlying EBITDA margins remained resilient at 50.3%,
with margins at an all-time high of 50.4% in Q2’27 – a 144 bps increase over the previous period. This strong
performance reflects the benefits of continued growth at scale, driving operating leverage and supporting margin
expansion, further reinforced by our cost efficiency programme.
• Profit after tax of $452m improved from $376m in the prior period reflecting the continued strength of the underlying
operating performance, despite higher finance costs and lower foreign exchange and derivative gains during the period.
In the current period, profit after tax was impacted by exceptional items of $55m relating to an in-principle settlement
in one of the Group’s subsidiaries and Airtel Money IPO expenses.
• Basic EPS increased to 10.2 cents from 8.3 cents. Excluding the impact of the exceptional items and the lower foreign
exchange and derivative gains in the current period, EPS increased to 11.8 cents from 6.9 cents.
Capital allocation
• Capex of $657m during the period more than doubled reflecting our commitment to accelerate capex spend to
reinforce our market position and support sustainable and profitable growth. During the period, we added
approximately 4,200 sites, bringing forward investment to enhance coverage and capacity, while supporting the
development of new businesses, including home broadband (HBB).
• Leverage has improved from 2.1x to 1.6x, with lease-adjusted leverage also improving to 0.5x from 0.8x in the previous
period, primarily driven by the improvement in underlying EBITDA.
• The Board has declared an interim dividend of 3.1 cents per share, an increase of 9.2%, in line with our dividend policy.
Furthermore, we returned $108.7m to shareholders through our share buyback programme during the period.

“The recent IPO of Airtel Money represents a significant milestone for the Group. We retained our shareholding through
the IPO and remain a long-term strategic shareholder, with significant exposure to Airtel Money’s future growth and value

underlying demand across our markets and consistent execution of our strategy, with total customer growth remaining
strong and stable at 11.6%. The investment case remains as compelling as ever, supported by favourable demographic
and economic trends, strong demand for data and financial services, and smartphones on our network increasing to over
100 million, with substantial headroom for further adoption. We are increasingly leveraging AI across the business to
deepen customer understanding, accelerate innovation, simplify customer journeys and strengthen the detection of fraud
and emerging threats. Combined with our ability to utilise our network, distribution and customer relationships to develop
new businesses, these structural drivers underpin our confidence in the Group’s long-term growth prospects.

margin of 50.4% in Q2’27 through a combination of operational discipline, ongoing cost efficiency programmes and
continued scale benefits. Looking ahead, margin performance will continue to be influenced by the macroeconomic
environment, including its impact on fuel costs. Our confidence in the growth trajectory of the business and the significant
long-term opportunity across our markets underpins our decision to accelerate investment. During the period, we
increased the pace of network investment to expand capacity and enhance service quality, positioning the business to
capture an increasing share of future growth.

markets, supported by sustained customer acquisition, increasing transaction volumes and ongoing expansion of its
product ecosystem. Our mobile money and mobile services businesses will continue to work closely together to capture
shared growth opportunities, realise operational synergies and deliver enhanced value for customers across our markets.
Supported by a strong balance sheet and robust cash generation, we remain confident in the opportunities ahead. As more
customers embrace digital services and financial inclusion, our continued focus on execution and customer experience
positions us to deliver sustainable growth at scale and long-term value for shareholders.”

About Airtel Africa
Airtel Africa is a leading provider of telecommunications and mobile money services, with operations in 14 countries in sub-
Saharan Africa. Airtel Africa provides an integrated offer to its subscribers, including mobile voice and data services as well
as mobile money services both nationally and internationally.
The company's strategy is focused on providing a great customer experience across the entire footprint, enabling our
corporate purpose of transforming lives across Africa.

Financial review for the half year ended 30 September 2026
Revenue
Group revenue in reported currency increased by 28.2% to $3,823m, with constant currency growth of 20.8%. Reported
currency revenue growth was higher than constant currency growth reflecting currency appreciation in Nigeria and Zambia.
Constant currency revenue growth was driven by Nigeria growth of 27.0%, East Africa growth of 18.2% and a strong
performance in Francophone Africa, which saw revenue growth accelerate to 19.2% in the current period and over 20% in
quarter ended 30 September 2026.
Mobile services revenue of $3,141m increased by 25.9% in reported currency and by 18.9% in constant currency. Constant
currency growth was led by voice revenue growth of 11.1% and data revenue growth of 28.0%. Mobile money revenues
grew by 34.8% in reported currency and by 25.8% in constant currency, driven by customer growth of 22.4% to 60.9 million
and ARPU rising by 2.6% to $2.5.
In Nigeria, reported currency revenues grew by 45.0%, and by 27.0% in constant currency. Higher reported currency growth
compared to constant currency was due to appreciation of the Nigerian naira from a weighted average NGN/USD rate of
1,553 in prior period to NGN/USD 1,360 in current period. In East Africa, reported currency revenue grew by 24.8% which
is also higher as compared to 18.2% constant currency growth primarily due to significant appreciation in the Zambian
kwacha. Francophone Africa reported currency revenue growth was 19.6%, in line with constant currency revenue growth
of 19.2%.
Underlying EBITDA3
Reported currency underlying EBITDA grew by 32.8% to $1,921m while in constant currency underlying EBITDA increased
by 23.5%. Reflecting the continued success of our cost efficiency programme, underlying EBITDA margins have increased

Strategic overview
The Group provides telecom and mobile money services in 14 markets across Africa. Our markets are characterised by a
young and rapidly growing population, low smartphone penetration and a large unbanked population. Unique mobile user
penetration across the Group’s footprint is under 50% and banking penetration remains under 40%. This illustrates the
significant opportunity ahead to deepen digital and financial inclusion across the communities we serve, enriching and
transforming our customers' lives through digitisation while driving profitable growth across our key services of voice, data
and mobile money
The Group continues to invest in its network and distribution infrastructure to enhance both mobile connectivity and
financial inclusion across our OpCos. We continue to invest in expanding our 4G and 5G networks to increase data capacity
and further enhance coverage across our markets.
Our strategy places customers at the heart of everything we do. By consistently delivering a superior customer experience,
we continue to fulfil our purpose of transforming lives across Africa. Our consumer centric strategy is anchored on six
strategic pillars: strengthening our ‘go-to-market’, delivering best in class network experience, winning more in key
markets, digitising and simplifying processes across the business, accelerating Airtel Money and scaling our home
broadband business (HBB) and enterprise offerings.
Underpinning the Group’s business strategy is our focus on cost optimisation, our sustainability strategy and the ongoing
investment into our people to build and retain talent. Our sustainability strategy supports our goals and commitments to
foster financial inclusion, bridge the digital divide and serve more customers in some of the least penetrated telecom’s
markets in the world.
Strengthen ‘Go-to-market’
We continued to expand and strengthen our distribution footprint across both urban and rural markets, with a particular
focus on our exclusive kiosk and mini-shop network, Airtel Money branches (AMB), and multi-brand outlets. During the
year, our mobile money business added more than 500,000 Airtel Money agents and 105,000 activating outlets, further
deepening our presence in towns and villages. This enhanced distribution has supported continued customer acquisition
and contributed to strong revenue growth across the business.
Alongside the expansion of our physical distribution infrastructure, we continued to invest in digital capabilities that simplify
processes and improve productivity for both our frontline sales teams and channel partners, helping drive greater
operational efficiency and scalability.
Data revenue growth continued to be supported by rising smartphone adoption and improving customer monetisation.
Smartphone penetration increased by 4.8 percentage points to 51.6%, driven by the ongoing expansion of our 4G and 5G
networks and strong commercial execution. We also remain focused on extending network reach through our strategic
partnership with Starlink which will help connect currently underserved rural communities and further strengthen the
quality and coverage of our network.
Brilliant network experience
Our network remains a source of competitive advantage, and we continue to strengthen our core infrastructure to build a
resilient, safe and reliable network to deliver a great customer experience to our customers. Over the period, we
accelerated our network investment to enhance 4G network availability and continued to expand our 5G network, including
the launch of 5G services in Gabon and the Seychelles, building on the strong progress already achieved across our existing
5G markets. Reaching underserved communities remains a key priority and we continue to expand rural coverage through
new site rollouts and investing in spectrum and technologies to support increased capacity to facilitate our corporate
purpose of transforming lives.
We deployed 4,200 infrastructure sites over the last year, alongside more than 4,400 additional 4G sites. As a result, 99.2%
of our network sites are now 4G-enabled while our 5G footprint has expanded to 4,100 operational sites across seven
markets.
As part of our commitment to building future-ready digital infrastructure, we continued to invest in spectrum, expand our
fibre network, enhance our 5G capabilities and progress our data centre investments. These investments support increasing
demand for connectivity and digital services while advancing digital inclusion across our markets. Our fibre business
continues to deliver encouraging revenue growth, with more than 2,000 km added during the year, bringing our total fibre
footprint to over 83,000 km.

Must win markets
Winning customers across all our markets through micro-marketing using network and digital tools is fundamental to our
strategy and continues to enable us to drive both financial and digital inclusion. We aim to win in every micro-segment by
optimising our network to improve the customer experience or strengthen our distribution where our network is already
strong, so that we can acquire new customers with speed and precision. There are clusters of opportunities which have
been identified across all OpCos which have been called out as ‘must win markets’. To ensure that we win across all ‘must
win markets’ we stepped up investment on building people capabilities and driving a culture of collaborative working across
functions.
In the broader urban areas, including smaller towns and emerging suburban peripheries, some micro-marketing actions
include improving indoor coverage, network quality and delivering a seamless customer experience by enhancing our
network through principles of community of interest. We are enhancing our in-store experience and increasing our own
store footprint, while strengthening 5G coverage in these markets to cater for rising home broadband (HBB) demand. This
will allow us to strengthen our position as a reliable network provider, attract new customers and lower churn.
Rural markets present a big growth opportunity to us, given the low penetration of both telecoms and financial services. To
tap the opportunity, our focus remains on improving coverage and distribution expansion across all markets. With
intensified network investment and focus on distribution excellence, we are confident that rural markets will contribute to
a significant portion of our overall customer additions going forward.
Digitise and simplify
Our digital strategy is bringing connectivity and financial services closer together, making it easier for customers to manage
their mobile needs and transact through Airtel Money in one experience. During H1’27, we continued to expand adoption
while sharpening our focus on how customers use our platforms - supporting everyday payments, repeat transactions and
greater engagement across services.
MyAirtel app is central to this approach, connecting our telecom and Airtel Money offerings through a single customer
interface. Monthly active users reached 14 million at the end of September 2026, compared with 8 million a year earlier.
Alongside this growth, we are focused on encouraging broader usage across telecom and financial services, extending the
app’s role from account management to a platform for customers’ everyday transactions.
In September 2026, monthly total payment value (TPV) processed through MyAirtel app exceeded $1.4bn, marking a new
milestone in the scale of our digital transaction activity. For the six months ended 30 September 2026, app TPV reached
$7.7bn, an increase of 75% year-on-year, while transacting users grew by 87%. These results demonstrate the increasing
importance of the app as a transaction channel across both telecom and Airtel Money services.
Airtel Money remains a key part of this integrated experience. Customers can transfer money, pay bills and merchants, and
purchase airtime and bundles within the same app. We continued to extend customer access through complementary
channels, including WhatsApp, supported by multilingual experiences across our markets. More targeted, data-led
engagement is helping customers navigate onboarding, complete their first transaction and discover additional services
relevant to their needs. This strengthens our focus on sustained usage and retention as digital adoption expands.
Across the Group, shared platforms and coordinated product roadmaps enable capabilities to be developed and deployed
across multiple markets. Combined with the sharing of successful customer engagement practices, this approach supports
faster execution, reduces duplication and allows operating companies to focus on customer adoption and commercial
performance. The progress achieved during HY’27 reinforces digital’s role in expanding the reach and usage of Airtel Money,
connecting financial services with our telecom offering and supporting a more efficient operating model across our markets.
Accelerate Airtel Money
Limited access to formal financial services, constrained banking infrastructure and continued reliance on cash across our
markets present a significant opportunity to accelerate financial inclusion. Airtel Money is addressing this through a digital-
first, mobile-led platform. Our focus remains on growing the customer base, scaling digital adoption and expanding our
distribution network and product ecosystem, including merchant and enterprise payments. On 14 October 2026, Airtel
Mobile Commerce N.V., the holding company of Airtel Money, was admitted to the Main Market of the London Stock
Exchange.

Risk factors
The risk factors summarised below relate to the Group’s business and industry in which it operates. Additional risks
and uncertainties relating to the Group that are currently unknown to the Group, or those the Group currently deems
immaterial, may, individually or cumulatively, also have a material adverse impact on the Group’s business, results of
operations and financial position. The Group’s principal and emerging risks and risk management process are
described in our Annual Report and Accounts 2026. Based on the Group’s assessment, there has been no changes to
the Group’s principal risks in the period.
Summary of principal risks
The Group continually monitors its external and internal environment to identify risks which have the ability to impact
its operations, financial performance or the achievement of its objectives.
1. We operate in a competitive environment with the potential for aggressive competition by existing players,
or the entry of new players, which could both put a downward pressure on prices, adversely affecting our
revenue and profitability.
2. Failure to innovate through simplifying the customer experience, developing adequate digital touchpoints in
line with changing customer needs and competitive landscape could lead to loss of customers and market
share.
3. Global geopolitical tensions and changes in macroeconomic conditions have the potential to impact our
business both directly and indirectly. These impacts include potential increases in the cost of our inputs and
negative effects on the disposable incomes of our customers, which could, in turn, affect sales and
profitability.
4. Cybersecurity threats through internal or external sabotage or system vulnerabilities could potentially result
in customer data breaches and/or service downtimes. This risk is increasing as AI-enabled attacks such as
automated phishing, bot-driven threats, and other AI-augmented intrusions grow more sophisticated and
harder to defend against.
5. Supply chain disruptions, whether affecting the Group directly or its key suppliers and partners, have the
potential to materially impact our ability to deliver products and services, increase operating costs and
negatively affect profitability. Risks arising from disruptions across global supply chains whether driven by
geopolitical instability, trade restrictions, natural disasters, or logistical constraints can cascade through our
supply chain and affect our operational continuity.
6. Shortages of skilled telecommunications professionals in some markets and the inability to identify and
develop successors for key leadership positions could both lead to disruptions in the execution of our
corporate strategy.
7. The resilience of our financial services platform is fundamental to achieving our strategic objectives and
advancing financial inclusion across our operating footprint. With the increasing scale of our financial services
business and the level of integration with third-party products, services and platforms, disruptions to platform
availability whether caused by technical failures, cybersecurity incidents, third-party system outages, or
infrastructure constraints can result in service interruptions that undermine customer trust, impact
transaction processing, and expose the Group to reputational and regulatory risk.
8. Our ability to provide quality of service to our customers and meet quality of service (QoS) requirements
depends on the robustness and resilience of our technology stack and ecosystem encompassing hardware,
software, products, services, applications and our ability to respond appropriately to any disruptions.
However, telecommunications networks are subject to the risks of technical failures, aging infrastructure,
human error, wilful acts of destruction or natural disasters.
19

9. We operate across diverse and dynamic legal, tax and regulatory environments. Adverse changes in the
political, macroeconomic and policy environment could negatively impact our ability to achieve our objectives.
While the Group makes every effort to comply with its legal and regulatory obligations across all operating
jurisdictions in line with its risk appetite, it remains continually exposed to an uncertain and evolving legal,
regulatory and policy environment in a number of its markets.
10. Our multinational footprint means we are constantly exposed to the risk of adverse currency fluctuations and
the macroeconomic conditions in the markets where we operate. We derive revenue and incur costs in local
currencies where we operate, but we also incur costs in foreign currencies, mainly from buying equipment
and services from manufacturers and technology service providers. That means adverse movements in
exchange rates between the currencies in our OpCos and the US dollar could have a negative effect on our
liquidity and financial condition. In some markets, we face instances of limited supply of foreign currency
within the local monetary system. This not only constrains our ability to fully benefit at Group level from strong
cash generation by those OpCos but also impacts our ability to make timely foreign currency payments to our
international suppliers.
Given the severity of this risk, specifically in some of our OpCos, the Group management continuously monitors the
potential impact of this risk of exchange rate fluctuations by comparing the average devaluation of each currency in
the markets in which the Group operates against US dollar on a ten-year historic basis and onshore forward exchange
rates over a one-year period, if available.
With respect to currency sensitivity going forward, over a 12-month period and assuming the movement occurs at the
beginning of the period, a further 1% movement of the USD against all OpCos currencies would result in an estimated
impact of $66m-$68m on revenues, $33m-$35m on EBITDA and $27m-$29m on finance cost (excluding derivatives). Our
largest exposure is to the Nigerian naira, where a similar 1% USD movement would result in an estimated $13m-$15m
impact on finance cost (excluding derivatives).
This does not represent any guidance and is being used solely to illustrate the potential impact of further currency
movements on the Group for the purpose of exchange rate risk management and assumes all other variables remain
constant. The accounting under IFRS is based on exchange rates in line with the requirements of IAS 21 ‘The Effect of
Changes in Foreign Exchange’ and does not factor in the devaluation mentioned above.
Based on the above-mentioned specific methodology for the identified OpCos, management evaluates specific mitigation
actions based on available mechanisms in each of the geographies. For further details on such mitigation action, refer to
the ‘Managing our risk’ section of the Annual Report and Accounts 2026 which can be downloaded from our website
www.airtel.africa

Alternative performance measures (APMs)
Introduction
In the reporting of financial information, the directors have adopted various APMs. These measures are not defined by
International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and therefore may not be directly comparable with other companies
APMs, including those in the Group’s industry.
APMs should be considered in addition to and are not intended to be a substitute for, or superior to, IFRS measurements.
Purpose
The directors believe that these APMs assist in providing additional useful information on the underlying trends,
performance and position of the Group.
APMs are also used to enhance the comparability of information between reporting periods and geographical units (such
as like-for-like sales), by adjusting for non-recurring or uncontrollable factors which affect IFRS measures, to aid users in
understanding the Group’s performance. Consequently, APMs are used by the directors and management for performance
analysis, planning, reporting and incentive-setting purposes.
The directors believe the following metrics to be the APMs used by the Group to help evaluate growth trends, establish
budgets and assess operational performance and efficiencies. These measures provide an enhanced understanding of the
Group’s results and related trends, therefore increasing transparency and clarity into the core results of the business.
