Telecommunication
31

The Nigerian telecommunications sector was shaped in 2025 by two major regulatory actions a critical 50% tariff
increase to offset FX pressures and forthcoming tax reform that simplifies the fiscal regime. These interventions
supported a financial recovery for key operators. The combined effect of these developments has stabilized the
sector's operational and financial foundation.
The Tariff Decision
The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) approved a 50% increase in telecoms tariffs — the first hike in nearly eleven
years. The move came as operators faced mounting losses from naira devaluation and rising operational costs.
Regulatory Rationale
Justified on grounds of elevated inflation, FX instability and capex pressures. The objective: restore financial sustainability and
preserve operators' capacity to invest in network quality and digital infrastructure.
Compliance Framework
Stricter obligations imposed on operators. Measurable network performance improvements mandated within 3 months.
Heightened oversight and more stringent penalties for non-compliance introduced.
Sector Outcome:
The tariff hike stabilised the sector's financial foundation, triggered an immediate positive signal to capital
markets, and marked a pivotal step toward re-establishing economics that support sustained investment in
connectivity infrastructure.

• Over 100,000 vehicles converted to CNG as at Q1 2025 (primarily commercial fleets).
• Concessional gas pricing at $1.57/MMBtu.
• Free or subsidised conversion kits under the Presidential CNG Initiative (PCNGI).
