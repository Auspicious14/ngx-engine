Interim sustainability disclosures
1. Introduction
1.1 Reporting entity and boundary
These interim sustainability disclosures form part of the unaudited condensed consolidated and separate interim
financial statements for the nine months ended 30 September 2026.
The reporting boundary is unchanged from H1 2026 and comprises MTN Nigeria Communications Plc and its subsidiaries,
including MoMo Payment Service Bank Limited and Yello Digital Financial Services Limited (the Fintech Companies) and XS
Broadband Limited, which were consolidated subsidiaries throughout the period.
The Structural Separation Transaction, approved by shareholders on 30 April 2026, had not been completed as at 30
September 2026 and remains subject to regulatory approvals. The Fintech Companies remain within the consolidation and
GHG emissions boundaries.
1.2. Sustainability disclosure policies
The Group’s sustainability disclosure policies are unchanged from the FY2025 Annual Report. Interim disclosures focus on
new information, events and circumstances and do not duplicate information previously reported in H1 2026 interim
disclosures.
1.3. Statement of compliance
These interim sustainability disclosures have been prepared by applying IFRS S1 General Requirements for Disclosure of
Sustainability-related Financial Information and IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures, as adopted for use in Nigeria,
together with applicable FRC Nigeria implementation guidance.
Quantified nine-month Scope 3 emissions are not presented for the reasons set out in Section 5.2.
The Board of Directors authorised both the unaudited condensed consolidated interim financial statements and these
interim sustainability disclosures for issue on 29 October 2026.
1.4. Time horizons
Unchanged: short term, 0 to 12 months; medium term, 1 to 3 years; long term, beyond 3 years.
1.5. Comparative information and approach to incrementality
These disclosures present incremental information arising in the quarter ended 30 September 2026 and cumulative nine-
month metrics. They update, and should be read together with, the FY2025 Annual Report and the H1 2026 interim
disclosures, which remain current except where stated here. Comparative interim information will be presented from 2027
where the corresponding prior interim period was prepared on a consistent basis.
1.6. Financial statement connectivity and cross-references
Cross-references to previously issued reports are used only where the referenced information is available to users on
the same terms and at the same time as these disclosures.

5.2. Cross-industry climate-related metrics
Scope 3 activity data is collated monthly and quarterly, but completeness checks, supplier validation and reconciliation are
performed through the annual Scope 3 measurement cycle. As at the reporting date, nine-month Scope 3 data was
incomplete and had not been internally validated. Quantifying Scope 3 emissions from this data would not be based on
reasonable and supportable information available at the reporting date without undue cost or effort and would not provide
reliable or decision-useful information. The FY2025 Scope 3 figure of 429,962 tCO2e remains the most recent reported
figure. Scope 3 emissions for 2026 will be measured through the annual cycle and disclosed in the FY2026 sustainability
disclosures.
Scope 1 and Scope 2 activity data is sourced from internal fuel, electricity, vehicle and project records. Emission factors are
selected for source specificity, geographic relevance, recency, data availability and consistency with the FY2025
methodology.

d) Translation to a Hyperinflationary Presentation Currency – Amendments to IAS 21
The amendments require translation from a non-hyperinflationary functional currency into a hyperinflationary
presentation currency at the closing rate.

currency of a hyperinflationary economy, its results and financial position are translated into the presentation currency by
translating all amounts (i.e., assets, liabilities, equity items, income and expenses) and all comparatives at the closing rate
at the date of the most recent statement of financial position.
An entity whose functional currency and presentation currency are the currency of a hyperinflationary economy, restates
the comparative amounts of a foreign operation, whose functional currency is that of a non-hyperinflationary economy, by
applying the general price index, in accordance with paragraph 34 of IAS 29, to the foreign operation’s comparative

Minimum tax disclosure:
The former minimum-tax provision under section 33 of the repealed Companies Income Tax Act (“CITA”) generally imposed minimum tax at 0.5% of gross turnover for companies
within its scope. CITA was repealed by the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (“NTA”), which took effect from 1 January 2026.
Section 57 of the NTA now provides for a minimum effective tax rate (“METR”) of 15%. Where a company’s NTA effective tax rate is below 15%, the company must recompute and
pay additional tax sufficient to bring the rate to 15%. The provision applies where the company:
• is a constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group with aggregate group turnover of at least £750 million or its equivalent; or
• has aggregate turnover of N50 billion or more in the relevant financial year.
For NTA purposes, the ETR is calculated as covered taxes divided by net income. Covered taxes include income tax and development levy, while net income is profit before tax
adjusted to exclude franked investment income and unrealised gains or losses. As at September 2026, MTNN minimum effective tax rate exceeded the 15% threshold. Hence, no
minimum tax liability.
Development Levy Disclosure:
Section 59 of the NTA imposes a development levy on the assessable profits of companies other than small companies and non-resident companies. The applicable rate is 4%.
Development levy is computed at 4% of assessable profit, before the deduction of losses and capital allowances used in arriving at total profit.

42. Fair value estimation
Where a financial asset or liability is carried on the statement of financial position at fair value, additional disclosure is
required. In particular, the fair values need to be classified in accordance with the fair value hierarchy. This fair value
hierarchy distinguishes between different fair value methodologies based on the level of subjectivity applied in the
valuation. The fair value hierarchy is split into the following levels:
Level 1: Quoted prices (unadjusted) in active markets for identical assets or liabilities (e.g. the price quoted on a stock
exchange for a listed share),
Level 2: Valuation techniques with inputs other than quoted prices (included within level 1) that are observable for the asset

the occurrence or non-occurrence of uncertain future events not wholly within the control of the Group in accordance with
IAS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets.
