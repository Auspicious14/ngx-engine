Lomé, 27 October 2026
Ecobank Reports 9M26 Profit Before Tax of $737 million; EPS of $0.0148
Revenue of $2.0 billion, up 12%; ROTE of 24.9%
Results Reflect Disciplined Execution of our GTR strategy, Improved Efficiency, and the Benefits of
Pan-African Diversification
• Profit attributable to ETI shareholders was $364 million, up 17%; EPS was up 15% to $0.0148 (1.48 US cents).
• Profit before tax (PBT) was $737 million, up 12%.
• Tangible book value per share (TBVPS) increased 25% to $0.0815 (8.15 US cents).
• Return on average assets (ROA) was 2.0%; Return on average tangible equity (ROTE) was 24.9%.
• Regional ROEs: CESA 35.9%; AWA 28.0%; UEMOA 22.4%; and NIGERIA 2.6%.
• Diversified revenue streams, with net revenue of $2.0 billion, up 12%, and 24.5% of total revenue driven by
stable, recurring fee and commission income.
• Payment revenue up 9% to $241 million, driven by wholesale payments, up 14% to $119 million, cards, up 5% to
$77 million, and remittances, up 25% to $8.1 million. Payment revenue accounts for 12% of total revenue.
• GTR strategy execution has improved efficiency and strengthened the franchise performance, with the cost-
to-income ratio (CIR) down 20 basis points to 47.8%. Customer deposits grew 14% to $27.5 billion, and CASA
deposits grew 16%.
• Capital position remains sound, with estimated Group Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio and Total Capital
Adequacy Ratio (CAR) ratio of 12.9% and 16.9% as of 30 September 2026.

Jeremy Awori, CEO of Ecobank Group, stated: “We achieved a profit before tax of $737 million, an increase of
12% year-on-year, and a return on tangible equity of 24.9%. Net revenue also grew by 12%, reaching $2.0 billion.”
Awori continued, “Our Growth, Transformation, and Returns strategy is clearly reflected in our efficiency and
franchise performance. Our cost-to-income ratio was 47.8%, ahead of our 2026 target range of 48% to 50%. Customer
deposits rose 14% to $27.5 billion, with current and savings accounts accounting for 85% of the total. This reflects the
confidence and trust our customers have in us and in using us for their day-to-day banking. This growth has helped
mitigate margin pressure from the rate-easing cycle in our markets, lifting net interest income by 12%. Payments
revenue also rose 9%, supported by our continued investment in technology and digital platforms. The strength of our
diversified business model was evident as profit before tax increased by 20% in Central Eastern and Southern Africa
(CESA) and by 10% in Anglophone West Africa (AWA), offsetting softer results in Francophone West Africa
(UEMOA) and Nigeria.”
Awori added, “Our non-performing loan (NPL) ratio remained broadly stable during the quarter at 7.5%, which is
within our 2026 target range of 6% to 8%, reflecting continued discipline in portfolio and risk management.”

In conclusion, Awori remarked, “Energy prices, inflation, and foreign exchange volatility are real risks as we move

Other Key Highlights.
• Customer deposits up 14% (up 14% in constant currency) to $27.5 billion. Current and savings account (CASA)

write-offs.

down 6% from year-end 2025, driven primarily by partial utilisation against loans written off in the Resolution
Vehicle.

Loans
Gross loans and advances to customers were $12.9 billion, up 6% (up 5% in constant currency). Year-to-

term loans, partially offset by trade loans. CCB loans were $4.3 billion, up 21% (up 16% in constant
currency), driven by microloans and personal loans.

Deposits and funding
Customer deposits were $27.5 billion, up 14% (up 14% in constant currency) and 9% year-to-date. CASA
deposits were $23.3 billion, or 84.5% of customer deposits, compared with 83.0% at 30 September 2025 and
83.3% at 31 December 2025.
CIB deposits were $11.7 billion, up 15% (up 18% in constant currency), driven by higher transaction
volumes and anchor relationships, particularly with international organisations. CCB deposits were $15.8
billion, up 13% (up 11% in constant currency), driven by primary banking relationships, a value-chain
strategy, more products held per customer, mobile and digital banking, and savings campaigns. UEMOA
deposits were $10.1 billion, up 8% (up 10% in constant currency). NIGERIA deposits were $2.9 billion, up
16% (up 8% in constant currency). AWA deposits were $6.8 billion, up 21% (up 18% in constant currency).
CESA deposits were $7.5 billion, up 17% (up 16% in constant currency).
The increase in CASA deposits reduced the cost of customer deposits by 15 basis points to 1.27%. The
average cost of funding decreased by 31 basis points to 1.97%. The loan-to-deposit ratio was 46.9%,
compared with 50.5%.

Asset quality
Non-performing loans were $971 million, or 7.5% of gross loans, compared with $1.2 billion, or 9.4%, at 31
December 2025 and $647 million, or 5.3%, at 30 September 2025. The $232 million year-to-date reduction
was driven mainly by the $200 million write-off of loans in the Resolution Vehicle, as well as collections,
recoveries, and staging upgrades, partially offset by interest accruals and stage migrations.
Accumulated ECL allowances were $942 million, up 13% and down 6% year-to-date, predominantly driven
by utilisation against the Resolution Vehicle write-off. The allowance includes $503 million of centrally held
ECL, built predominantly for risks in NIGERIA. Accumulated ECL was 7.3% of gross loans, compared with
7.8% at 31 December 2025 and 6.9% at 30 September 2025. The NPL coverage ratio was 97.0%, up from
83.3% at 31 December 2025, and the Stage 3 coverage ratio was 67.5%, up from 62.2% at 31 December
2025.

Capital and liquidity
As of 30 September 2026, the Group's equity attributable to ETI shareholders was $2.0 billion, up 6%
from $1.9 billion at 31 December 2025, driven primarily by profit attributable to ETI shareholders of $364
million, partially offset by foreign currency translation reserve (FCTR) losses and the $40 million dividend
payout related to 2025.
Ecobank’s capital ratios remained well above the minimum required by regulators. As of 30 September 2026,
the Group’s estimated capital adequacy ratios indicate that the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio is 12.9%,
the Tier 1 Capital ratio is 13.5%, and the Total Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) is 16.9%. The CET1 and Tier
1 CAR estimates for September 2026 remained largely stable compared to December 2025. During the year,
ETI issued a $450 million Tier 2 bond, replacing the previous $350 million Tier 2 bond, for a net increase of
$100 million in ETI's Tier 2 instruments. The Group also replaced $75 million of existing AT1 with a new
$70 million instrument, effectively reducing the Group's AT1 instruments by $5 million.
