INTERIM RESULTS FOR NINE MONTHS ENDED 30th SEPTEMBER 2026
Lagos, 28th October 2026
Record PAT of ₦974.8B, ₦1.8 trillion operating cash flow, and a historic swing to a ₦51.1B
net cash position
Lagos, 28th October 2026: Dangote Cement PLC (DANGCEM: NGX), Africa’s largest cement
producer, today announces interim results for the nine months ended 30th September 2026,
approved by the Board of Directors on 27th October 2026. The Group delivered robust double-
digit growth across all key metrics, record cash generation of ₦1.8 trillion from operations,
and a historic balance sheet transformation from a net debt position of ₦682.9B at the end
of 2025 to a net cash position of ₦51.1B¹.
Financial highlights - 9M 2026
• Group revenue up 19.6% to ₦3,774.2B
• Group EBITDA up 23.7% to ₦1,766.3B; 46.8% margin (9M 2025: 45.3%)
• Gross margin up 4.0 percentage points to 63.2%
• Nigeria EBITDA up 23.7% to ₦1,597.0B; 59.8% margin
• Profit after tax up 31.2% at ₦974.8B
• Earnings per share up 33.2% at ₦58.37
• Operating cash flow up 41.5% to ₦1,826.6B; free cash flow of ₦1.26 trillion covers dividends paid
of ₦753.8B by approximately 1.7 times
• Net cash balance of ₦51.1B¹
Operating highlights - 9M 2026
• Group volumes up by 9.4% to 22.1Mt
• Pan-African volumes rebounded strongly, up 14.0% year on year to 9.1Mt.
• Nigeria cement and clinker exports up 41.8% at 1.5Mt; 26 ships of clinker dispatched from Nigeria
to Ghana, Cameroon and Côte d’Ivoire
• Customer advances (contract liabilities) up 45% to ₦208.4B - a strong forward demand indicator
• Strong reduction in Nigeria cash cost due to favorable energy mix; fuel and power costs up only
2.0% against 9.4% volume growth
• 500 CNG trucks commissioned in Tanzania; alternative fuel utilization reached 30% in Zambia and
17.2% in Congo
• Commissioned Okpella mobile refueling unit
Capacity expansion
• 6Mta phase 1 Itori integrated plant on track for commissioning - a key step towards our medium-
term target of 80Mta installed capacity

Arvind Pathak, Chief Executive Officer, said:
“The Group delivered a record performance in the nine months ended September 2026. Revenue
rose 19.6% to ₦3.77 trillion, EBITDA rose 23.7% to ₦1.77 trillion at a 46.8% margin, and profit
after tax grew 31.2% to a record ₦974.8 billion - delivered through volume growth, broader
market reach and disciplined execution of our strategic priorities.
The defining achievement of this period is the transformation of our balance sheet. Through ₦1.8
trillion of operating cash flow - up 41.5% - and ₦539.0 billion loan repayments, we moved from a
net debt position of ₦682.9 billion to a net cash position of ₦51.1 billion in nine months. Dangote
Cement is now a net-cash company: we funded ₦566.7 billion of expansion capital expenditure
and a record ₦754 billion dividend entirely from internal cash generation. From here, with our
deleveraging complete, free cash flow is no longer absorbed by debt paydown - it is available for
growth and shareholder returns.
The quality of our earnings improved alongside the quantity. Gross margin expanded 4.0
percentage points to 63.2%, as production costs grew just 7.9% against revenue growth of
19.6%. Fuel and power costs rose only 2.0% despite 9.4% volume growth - clear evidence that
our energy-mix, alternative-fuel and CNG logistics investments are delivering real cash savings.
Demand indicators remain firmly positive. Customer advances rose 45% to ₦208.4 billion, exports
from Nigeria grew 41.8% on 26 clinker shipments, and our pan-African volumes rebounded
14.0%. Regional trade is becoming a growth engine, underscoring Nigeria’s role as a strategic
manufacturing and export base.
Our capacity expansion programme advanced significantly, with construction of the first phase of
the new 6Mta Itori plant in its final stage - a strategic, fully self-funded investment in future
growth and an important step towards our medium-term target of 80Mta installed capacity.
As we enter the final quarter, our priorities remain clear: sustain volume growth, protect margins
through cost discipline, expand our regional market presence, and complete our strategic capacity
investments. With record profitability, record cash generation and a net-cash balance sheet, we
approach these priorities from a position of strength unmatched among African cement
producers.”

Supported by resilient demand, a more stable naira and the Central Bank of Nigeria’s 350 basis point rate
cut in September, we expect:

About Dangote Cement
Dangote Cement is Africa's leading cement producer with 55.0Mta capacity across Africa. A fully integrated
quarry-to-customer producer, we have a production capacity of 35.25Mta in our home market, Nigeria.
Our Obajana plant in Kogi state, Nigeria, is the largest in Africa with 16.25Mta of capacity across five lines;
our Ibese plant in Ogun State has four cement lines with a combined installed capacity of 12Mta; our Gboko
plant in Benue state has 4Mta; and our Okpella plant in Edo state has 3Mta. Through our recent
investments, Dangote Cement has eliminated Nigeria's dependence on imported cement and has
transformed the nation into an exporter of cement and clinker, serving neighbouring countries.
In addition, we have operations in Cameroon (1.5Mta clinker grinding), Congo (1.5Mta), Ghana (2.0Mta clinker
grinding and import), Ethiopia (2.5Mta), Senegal (1.5Mta), Sierra Leone (0.5Mta import), South Africa
(2.8Mta), Tanzania (3.0Mta), Zambia (1.5Mta), Cote d’ Ivoire (3Mta clinker grinding).

Macroeconomic outlook
After navigating heightened trade barriers and uncertainty last year, the global economy now faces a
fresh shock from the war in the Middle East. Growth is projected to slow to 3.1% in 2026 before
recovering to 3.4% in 2027, weighed down by heightened geopolitical tensions and elevated energy
prices. Headline inflation is expected to rise in 2026 before resuming its downward trend in 2027, with
energy-importing economies particularly exposed to higher import costs and weaker real incomes.
Growth in Sub-Saharan Africa is projected to remain broadly stable at 4.5% in both 2026 and 2027.
However, the regional outlook masks significant differences across countries, with oil-importing, non-
resource-intensive economies facing greater pressure from higher energy and food prices. Regional
inflation is projected to moderate from 12.6% in 2025 to 8.6% in 2026, remaining at 8.6% in 2027.
Nigeria Region
In our financial reporting, the Nigerian region includes Dangote Cement Plc (‘the company’) which has
plants in Obajana, Ibese and Gboko; DCP Cement Ltd with a 3Mt plant in Obajana; and Okpella Cement
Plc’s 3Mt plant.
Nigeria’s macroeconomic environment improved during the nine-month period, supported by a more
stable foreign exchange regime, stronger foreign exchange reserves and moderating inflation. Improved
investment flows, following ongoing government reforms, supported activity in critical sectors including
mining, oil and gas, and infrastructure development. The latest available official inflation reading was
14.36% year-on-year in September 2026, the lowest since the National Bureau of Statistics overhauled
the methodology in January 2025. Additionally, the Central Bank of Nigeria reduced the Monetary Policy
Rate by 350 basis points to 23.0% at its September 2026 meeting, its biggest single rate cut in its
history to support growth.
Although inflation moderated during the latter part of the period, operating costs remained elevated
due to higher fuel, energy, logistics and spare-parts expenses. Rising fuel prices increased
transportation costs, further constraining consumer purchasing power.
Against this backdrop, our Nigerian sales volumes grew by 6.4% to 14.1Mt in 9M 2026, from 13.2Mt in
the corresponding period of 2025. Growth was primarily driven by increased government infrastructure
projects, housing development, a stronger private-sector project pipeline and investment in bulk
tankers.
Consequently, revenue from our Nigerian operations rose by 22.5% to ₦2,670.9B in 9M 2026, while
EBITDA was up by 23.7% to ₦1,597.0B, excluding central costs and eliminations (9M 2025: ₦1,291.4B,
margin of 59.2%). The Nigeria EBITDA margin was 59.8%.
During the period, the Nigeria region shipped 1.0Mt of clinker to Cameroon, Ghana and Cote d’Ivoire,
up by 35.4% from the corresponding period last year. Cumulatively, total Nigerian exports (cement +
clinker) were up 41.8% to 1.5Mt
Pan-Africa Region
The pan-African region includes all operations outside Nigeria.
Our Pan-African operations reported a strong rebound in volumes, up by 14.0% to 9.1Mt in 9M 2026,
compared to 7.9Mt in 9M 2025. This growth was driven by increase in government spending,
infrastructure investment and services expansion, moderate inflation and stabilisation of other macro-
economic variables.
Consequently, total pan-African revenue was up by 12.6% to ₦1,189.8B in 9M 2026 from ₦1,056.6B in
9M 2025, while EBITDA recorded a double-digit growth of 12.7% to ₦226.7B (before central costs and
eliminations) in 9M 2026.

Cameroon
Cameroon’s GDP growth is forecast to grow 3.2% in 2026, down from 3.5% in 2025.
Cameroon’s cement sector faced a slower start to 2026, as post-election dynamics constrained activity
in the construction sector. Delays in government spending and the slower rollout of infrastructure
projects weighed on overall demand, tempering the contribution of the industrial sector to GDP growth
in the quarter.
Macroeconomic conditions, however, remained relatively supportive. Inflation continued to ease,
moderating slightly to 3.4% in 2026. This softer inflation environment helped preserve purchasing
power and provided a degree of stability for businesses, albeit a slow sectoral activity.
Against this backdrop, Dangote Cement Cameroon recorded a subdued performance in 9M 2026, with
sales volumes declining 9.8% to 836.8Kt. Performance in the period was impacted by production
disruptions arising from logistical challenges that constrained depot replenishment
Congo
Congo’s GDP is projected to grow at 3.5% in 2026, faster than the 2.4% growth in 2025.
Dangote Cement Congo strengthened its market position in 9M 2026, benefiting from robust domestic
demand and increased sales to neighbouring markets. Construction activity was supported by ongoing
private developments and infrastructure projects, including road rehabilitation, port expansion, hospitals
and commercial developments, while GDP growth and inflation stood at 3.6% and 3%, respectively.
Sales from our 1.5Mta Mfila plant rose by 11.9% to 790.2Kt, compared with 706.2Kt in 9M 2025, with
market share increasing to 73% from 63%. Performance was supported by both domestic and export
demand, despite temporary disruption from the presidential election, which weighed on sales during
the campaign period.
Operationally, alternative fuel substitution increased to 17.2%, following increased waste-oil utilisation,
reducing reliance on conventional fuels and supporting cost efficiency.
Ethiopia
Ethiopia’s GDP projection is anticipated at 9.2% in 2026, flat from 2025.
Ethiopia’s cement demand remained supported in 9M 2026 by strong economic activity, population
growth, urbanisation and investment in infrastructure, housing and industrial projects. However,
inflation, currency depreciation, tight financing conditions and fuel and FX constraints continued to
weigh on affordability and construction activity. Headline inflation, though moderated to 14.9% in
September, remains elevated relative to the monetary policy rate was 16%.
Against this backdrop, sales volume from our 2.5Mta Mugher plant increased by 18.8% to 1.9Mt in 9M
2026, from 1.6Mt in the corresponding period of 2025, despite an 11% contraction in the overall market.
Growth was supported by improved production reliability, product availability and strong market
execution, with market share increasing to 33% from 25%.
Looking ahead, demand is expected to be supported by road and corridor projects, housing and urban
development, industrial projects and aviation infrastructure, including the planned Bishoftu airport
development.

Ghana
Ghana’s GDP growth is projected to grow at a slow pace of 5.7% in 2026, down from 6.0% growth in
2025
Despite a supportive medium-term demand outlook driven by infrastructure investment, housing
development and private-sector construction, Ghana's cement market remained intensely competitive
in 9M 2026. Aggressive pricing strategies by incumbent producers and new entrants continued to
pressure volumes and margins across the industry, while supply chain constraints affected product
availability.
Sales volumes declined by 10.1% to 276.1Kt, compared with 307.7Kt in the corresponding period of
2025. Looking ahead, major projects including the Accra-Kumasi Expressway, Big Push Infrastructure
Programme, Tema-Aflao Road and various housing developments are expected to support cement
demand, although competition is likely to remain a key feature of the operating environment.
Senegal
Senegal’s GDP growth is projected to moderate to 2.2% in 2026 from a growth of 6.7% in 2025.
Senegal’s cement market showed continued signs of recovery during the first nine months of 2026,
supported by the gradual resumption of previously suspended projects. Cement demand benefited from
the partial reopening of previously suspended projects, although the market continued to be influenced
by project execution timelines and the broader socio-political environment. Despite challenges in Mali,
Senegal’s main export market, demand was partially offset by increased exports to other regional
markets.
Dangote Cement Senegal delivered a strong performance, with sales volumes increasing by 29.9% to
1.1Mt in 9M 2026, compared to 852.4Kt in the corresponding period of 2025. Growth was driven by the
gradual market recovery that commenced in Q4 2025, strong commercial execution, particularly in the
domestic market, and improved logistics conditions that supported higher export volumes.
South Africa
South Africa’s GDP growth is forecasted to moderate to 1.0% in 2026, from 1.1% in 2025.
South Africa’s economic environment remained challenging during the first nine months of 2026, with
weak economic growth and subdued investment activity weighing on construction demand. GDP
contracted by 0.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 2026, while gross fixed capital formation declined,
including a 4.0% reduction in construction works. Higher inflation, elevated borrowing costs and a sharp
increase in fuel prices also pressured consumer spending and construction activity.
Sales volumes from our South African operation declined by 6.1% during the period, reflecting weak
market demand, reduced construction activity and increased competition from imported cement. The
operation continues to be managed for cash generation and cost discipline.
Tanzania
Tanzania’s GDP is projected to grow at a faster pace of 6.0% in 2026 from 5.9% in 2025.
Tanzania’s economy remained robust during the first nine months of 2026, supported by strong growth
in mining, tourism, agriculture and infrastructure development. Cement demand continued to benefit
from major projects, including the Standard Gauge Railway, EACOP and various housing and port
developments. Inflation remained relatively stable at 4.5% in September 2026.
Dangote Cement Tanzania delivered a strong performance, with sales volumes increasing by 9.2% to
1.8Mt in 9M 2026, compared with 1.6Mt in the corresponding period of 2025. Growth was driven by
continued market expansion and supported by increase in operational efficiency through additional
investments in CNG trucks to support our logistics network.

Zambia
Zambia’s GDP is forecasted to grow by 5.6% in 2026, faster than the growth of 3.8% in 2025.
Zambia’s economy remained resilient during the first nine months of 2026, supported by increased
investment in the mining, energy and agriculture sectors. Cement demand benefited from higher copper
mining activity, ongoing infrastructure projects and increased construction across supporting industries.
Inflation moderated significantly during the period, declining from 9.4% at the start of the year to 6.1%
in September, while the Monetary Policy Rate was reduced from 14.25% to 10.75%, supporting
economic activity. Despite this improvement, input costs remained elevated due to external factors,
including higher energy costs linked to the Middle East conflict.
Sales volume at our 1.5Mta Ndola factory declined marginally by 0.7% to 669.7Kt in 9M 2026, compared
to 674.3Kt in the corresponding period of 2025.
To improve operational efficiency, we increased alternative fuel utilization to an average of 30% in the
third quarter, supporting cost optimization and operational performance during the period
Côte d’Ivoire
Côte d’Ivoire’s GDP growth is projected to moderate to 6.0% in 2026, down from 6.5% 2025.
In the nine-month period of 2026, Côte d’Ivoire’s macroeconomic environment remained stable,
supported by low inflation anchored at about 2% in 2025 and sustained GDP growth averaging 7%
over the past decade. This stability helped preserve consumer purchasing power and contain input cost
pressures. Political conditions also remained steady following the October 2025 presidential election,
with no disruption to economic activity.
Dangote Cement Côte d’Ivoire recorded a solid nine-month performance, with its newly commissioned
grinding plant delivering sales volumes of 529.2Kt as production ramp-up continued, supporting overall
volume growth despite intensifying market competition.
