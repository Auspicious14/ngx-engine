9M 2026 Unaudited
Results Release
30 October 2026

Lagos, Nigeria | 30 October 2026 – Oando PLC (“Oando” or the “Group”), Africa’s
leading indigenous energy group listed on both the Nigerian Exchange and
Johannesburg Stock Exchange, today announces its unaudited results for the nine
months ended 30 September 2026.
Operational delivery drives operating profit, cash and deleveraging
Group Highlights
• Average production of 42,985 boepd, up 14% year-on-year and within
guidance, driven by new wells and higher uptime.
• Production Opex down 22% to $15.39/boe (9M 2025: $19.67/boe), driven by
sustained delivery of the Group’s cost optimisation initiatives across the
enlarged asset base
• Facility uptime of 96.1% (9M 2025: 85%), with zero fatalities and zero lost-time
injuries across 14.9 million hours worked
• Traded crude volumes of 17.5 MMbbl across 32 cargoes (9M 2025: 19.8 MMbbl
across 21 cargoes), with increased sourcing from marginal field producers.
• NGL delivery of 9.5 kbpd (gross), the highest in five years, and an 80% increase
in gas supply to the JV-owned Okpai IPP, supporting higher power generation
for the national grid
• Revenue up 9% to ₦2,776.1 billion (9M 2025: ₦2,541.5 billion), led by E&P
growth on higher crude oil liftings and gas and NGL sales, alongside improved
average realised prices
• Operating profit of ₦159.7 billion (9M 2025: operating loss of ₦109.7 billion), on
higher production and the absence of a prior-period fair value loss
• Adjusted EBITDA of ₦181.8 billion (9M 2025: negative ₦194.1 billion), driven
by higher E&P sales volumes and realised prices
• Profit after tax of ₦106.5 billion (9M 2025: ₦201.3 billion), held back by ₦244.2
billion of finance costs on acquisition debt
• Cash generated from operations of ₦285.0 billion (9M 2025: ₦339.2 billion
used)
• Capex of ₦110.7 billion (9M 2025: ₦85.1 billion), mainly for drilling across OMLs
60–63
• Closing cash of ₦175.9 billion (9M 2025: ₦143.8 billion); total borrowings down
22% from the FY 2024 peak to ₦2.16 trillion, with the Corporate Facility and
Medium-Term Loan restructured and in good standing
Commenting on the results, Wale Tinubu CON, Group Chief Executive, Oando
PLC, said:
“Our performance in the first nine months of 2026 demonstrates how diligent execution
under our operatorship, supported by our renewed investment drive, is delivering
greater efficiency, improved reliability, lower unit costs and ultimately higher production
across our expanded upstream portfolio. Production averaged 42,985 boepd, up 14%
year-on-year, while facility uptime improved to 96.1% and production operating costs

Operations Review
E&P Business Performance
Production
Production for the period was within guidance, averaging 42,985 boepd, up 14% from the 9M
2025 average. Unit costs continued to fall. Production Opex was $15.39/boe, down 22% from
$19.67/boe in 9M 2025, reflecting sustained savings from the Group’s cost optimisation
initiatives, notably in transport, logistics, services and ICT, and higher production across a
largely fixed field cost base.

OMLs 60–63 (40% WI, Operator)
Production from OMLs 60–63 averaged 40,017 boepd in 9M 2026, up 16% year-on-year (9M
2025: 34,499 boepd), with growth across all three streams: crude oil rose 14% to 10,600 bopd,
gas 15% to 27,314 boepd and NGLs 53% to 2,103 bpd. The increase was driven by new Idu
wells brought onstream in May and August, the restart of Samabri 4T following an ESP
replacement and the return to service of the Oshie flow station in June.
Rich gas from Oshie and remediation work on the NGL recovery unit supported a JV NGL
delivery of 9.5 kbpd to Indorama, the highest in five years. Completion of the Ob/Ob-Kwale
reverse flow project increased gas supply to the JV-owned Okpai IPP by 80%, enabling higher
power generation for the national grid and strengthening the Group’s domestic gas-to-power
position.
OML 56 – Ebendo (45% WI)
Average daily production was broadly flat at 2,585 boepd (9M 2025: 2,574 boepd). In June
2026, the Group agreed to sell its interest in OML 56 (now PML 23) to Energia Limited; see
Assets Held for Sale in the Financial Review.
OML 13 – Qua Ibo (40% WI)
Production averaged 384 bopd in 9M 2026, down 29% from 541 bopd in 9M 2025, primarily
due to natural field decline.

Drilling Programme
The Group’s seven-well drilling campaign for 2026 across its operated OMLs 60–63 has
progressed in line with plan. Of the first six wells, Idu 6ST and Idu 15 are complete and on
stream; workovers at Samabri 4T and 7T are complete, and Idu T and Samabri A50 are
currently being drilled. The seventh and final well, Ogbainbiri, is scheduled to spud in
December 2026. On completion, the campaign is expected to add approximately 16,000–
20,000 boepd (gross) of production, helping to sustain plateau output and offset natural field
decline across the operated portfolio.
Exploration – Block KON 13 (Angola)
Through its subsidiary, Oando Exploration and Production Angola Ltd, the Company holds a
45% participating interest and operates KON 13. This lies in the onshore part of the Kwanza
Basin in the Republic of Angola. Following the signing of the Production Sharing Contract in
March this year, the Contractor Group, led by Oando, continues technical studies and plans
a 2D seismic acquisition programme.

financing structure; it does not reflect any change in the Group’s underlying trading capability
or commercial strategy.

Revenue
Group revenue grew 9% YoY to ₦2,776.1 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025: ₦2,541.5 billion), driven
primarily by growth in the Exploration & Production segment, supported by a 21% rise in total
E&P sales volumes to 10.21 MMboe (9M 2025: 8.45 MMboe) and elevated crude oil prices
amid ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Growth in naira terms was held back
by the stronger naira, which averaged ₦1,366/$ (9M 2025: ₦1,528/$). Key performance
drivers included:
• Crude Oil liftings rose 43% to 3.97 MMbbl (9M 2025: 2.77 MMbbl), alongside a 31%
rise in the average realised price to $87.31/bbl (9M 2025: $66.84/bbl). This contributed
₦401 billion in revenue, up 84% year-on-year.
• Natural Gas sales volumes increased 5% to 34.01 Bscf (9M 2025: 32.28 Bscf), and
the average realised gas price increased by 46% to $2.26/Mscf (9M 2025: $1.55/Mscf).
This contributed ₦103 billion in revenue, up 45% year-on-year.
• Natural Gas Liquids (NGLs) sales volumes increased 90% to 0.57 MMbbl (9M 2025:
0.30 MMbbl), and the average realised NGL price increased by 28% to $8.80/bbl (9M
2025: $6.85/bbl). This contributed ₦6 billion in revenue, up 50% year on year.
• Trading revenue rose 1% to ₦2,219.5 billion (9M 2025: ₦2,188.6 billion), reflecting a
higher price environment.
Gross Profit
Gross profit increased by 483% to ₦174.5 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025: ₦29.8 billion), as
higher crude oil prices and sales volumes drove revenue growth ahead of production cost
growth. While the group continued to benefit from cost optimisation initiatives across the
enlarged asset base, the modest 3.6% increase in cost of sales was attributable to the impact
of the reclassification of employee benefit expenses and depletion, depreciation and
amortisation (DD&A) of oil and gas assets from administrative expenses to cost of sales during
the period.
Administrative Expenses
Administrative expenses increased by 21% to ₦157.9 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025: ₦130.4
billion), driven primarily by a 60% increase in professional fees to ₦79.0 billion (9M 2025:
₦49.4 billion), mainly reflecting advisory costs on the Group’s debt restructuring and capital
management programme, which are not expected to recur once the programme completes.

Operating Profit/(Loss)
The Group reported an operating profit of ₦159.7 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025 operating loss:
₦109.7 billion). This reflects growth in gross profit to ₦174.5 billion, a swing to other operating
income of ₦80.7 billion (9M 2025 other operating loss: ₦160.0 billion), and a net impairment
reversal on financial assets of ₦62.3 billion (9M 2025: ₦150.7 billion). The swing in other
operating income was mainly driven by the absence of the ₦311.6 billion fair value loss on
modification of financial assets recognised in 9M 2025, along with higher net foreign exchange
gains and a profit on an asset disposal. The impairment reversal was 59% lower year-on-year,
driven by settled customer receivables and a nil finance lease impairment in Q3 2026.
Adjusted EBITDA was ₦181.8 billion (9M 2025: negative ₦194.1 billion). The improvement
reflects the growth in gross profit and other operating income and excludes the effect of
impairment reversals in both periods.
Net Finance Income/(Costs)
Net finance cost stood at ₦235.2 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025 net finance income: ₦128.0
billion). This was driven by lower finance income earned in the period at ₦9.0 billion (9M 2025:
₦368.7 billion), as 9M 2025 had benefited from non-recurring interest income of ₦321.1 billion
on loan receivables and bank deposits and ₦47.6 billion on finance leases. Finance costs rose
slightly to ₦244.2 billion (9M 2025: ₦240.7 billion), following principal repayments in the
period. With borrowings still elevated after the acquisition, debt service absorbs a substantial
share of the cash the business generates: interest paid during the period was ₦143.9 billion,
equivalent to 50% of cash generated from operations. Reducing this burden is a principal
objective of the capital management programme set out on pages 10 and 11.
Taxation
The Group recognised a tax credit of ₦181.1 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025: ₦181.8 billion),
arising from reversing provisions previously recognised for Companies Income Tax,
Petroleum Profit Tax, Capital Gains Tax, and Education Tax.
Profit After Tax
Profit after tax fell 47% to ₦106.5 billion in 9M 2026 (9M 2025: ₦201.3 billion), as the swing
from net finance income to net finance costs more than offset the improvement in operating
profit. Both periods included a similar tax credit (9M 2026: ₦181.1 billion; 9M 2025: ₦181.8
billion). The year-on-year decline therefore reflects financing costs and comparative effects
rather than underlying operating performance. Earnings per share declined 25% to ₦12 (9M
2025: ₦16).
Cash Flow
Net cash generated from operating activities was ₦217.8 billion in 9M 2026, compared to
₦235.0 billion used in 9M 2025. This reflects a positive swing in cash generated from
operations before interest and tax to ₦285.0 billion (9M 2025: ₦339.2 billion outflow), a
working capital inflow of ₦77.0 billion (9M 2025: ₦272.5 billion inflow), and interest payments
of ₦143.9 billion (9M 2025: ₦165.2 billion).

more of the Group’s operating performance is expected to flow through to profit after tax, and
a greater share of operating cash flow will be available for reinvestment and, in time, for
distribution to shareholders.
Hedging
To manage oil price volatility and support revenue stability, the Group implemented a hedging
programme covering 6,000 barrels per day using purchased put options at a strike price of
$59/bbl. These instruments provide downside protection while preserving upside exposure.
Assets Held for Sale
In June 2026, the Group agreed to sell its interest in OML 56 (now PML 23), held through
OPDC, to Energia Limited for $48.45 million subject to closing adjustments, and OPDC’s net
assets are classified as held for sale. OML 56 contributed 2,585 boepd in 9M 2026, about 6%
of Group production, and accounts for 4.6 MMboe (0.5%) of the Group’s 2P reserves. The
sale supports the Group’s strategy to exit non-core assets and focus capital on its core assets.
