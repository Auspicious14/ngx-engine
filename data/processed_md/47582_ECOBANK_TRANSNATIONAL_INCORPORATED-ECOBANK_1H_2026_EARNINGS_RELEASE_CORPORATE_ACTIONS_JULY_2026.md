Ecobank Reports 1H 2026 Profit Before Tax of $423m,
Delivered Resilient Net Revenue Growth of 15% to $1.3bn & ROTE of 21.1%
Half-Year Results Reflect Effective Execution of our GTR strategy, Disciplined Operational Efficiency,
Pan-African Diversification Benefits, and a Relentless Focus on the Customer
• Attributable profit to the shareholders of ETI was $198m, up 2%, with EPS at $0.0080 (0.80 US cents).
• Profit before tax (PBT) was $423m, up 6% year-on-year (YoY).
• Tangible book value per share (TBVPS) of $0.075 (7.51 US cents), increased 24% YoY.
• Return on average assets (ROA) of 1.7%; Return on average tangible equity (ROTE) of 21.1%
• Regional ROEs: CESA 35.4%; AWA 29.0%, UEMOA 22.7%, and NIGERIA 3.0%.
• Net revenue of $1.3bn, up 15%, with 41.4% of net revenue generated from stable, recurring non-interest revenue.
• Payment revenue up 10% to $156m (accounts for 12% of net revenues), driven by a 14% increase in
disbursements (wholesale payments) to $77m, 6% growth in card-related fees to $49m, and a 26% increase in
merchant solutions fees to $11m.
• Cost-to-income (CIR) of 48.4% improved from 49.1% in 1H25.
• Value of digital transactions increased by 33%, to $78.5bn, with transaction volumes flat at 118m during the first
half of the year.
• Capital position remains sound, with estimated Group Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) and Total Capital
Adequacy Ratio (CAR) ratios of 13.3% and 17.4% as of 30 June 2026.

Net revenue, which is the sum of net interest income (NII) and non-interest revenue (NIR), increased by 15%
to $1.3 billion during the first half of 2026 compared to the same period in the previous year. Net revenue in
the Corporate and Investment Banking (CIB) segment rose by 23% to $709 million, primarily due to
effective balance sheet, treasury, liquidity management, and increasing advisory and capital markets
mandates. This increase was particularly supported by the rise in excess liquidity resulting from strong
deposit generation. In the Consumer and Commercial Banking (CCB) segment, net revenue grew by 6% to
$609 million. The Consumer Banking segment experienced a 9% increase in net revenue, reaching $286
million, driven by higher income from deposits and microloans—especially in Ghana—as well as credit card
transactions and digital payments. In the Commercial Banking segment, net revenue rose by 4% to $323
million, driven by trade finance loans and loans supported by Development Finance Institutions (DFIs) for
small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), women-led businesses, and agribusinesses.
Net interest income (NII), defined as the difference between the interest income earned on interest-earning
assets and the interest expenses incurred on interest-bearing liabilities, increased by 20%, to $751 million
during the first half of 2026 compared to the same period last year. This growth was driven by a 15% rise in
interest income and a 6% increase in interest expenses.
The growth in NII was supported by higher margins resulting from an increase in low-cost CASA (current
account savings account) deposits and a decrease in the cost of deposits. Additionally, effective balance sheet
management and treasury and liquidity solutions, along with growth in term loans, trade finance, and
microloans across various business segments, contributed to this improvement.
The net interest margin (NIM) stood at 5.7% for the first half of 2026, which reflects an increase of 11 basis
points compared to the previous year. This rise was largely attributed to a 27-basis point reduction in the
average interest rate paid on interest-bearing liabilities, which fell to 2.3%. This reduction was driven by the
growth and mix of deposits, as well as lower costs.
Non-interest revenue (NIR) increased by 8% to $531 million during the first half of 2026. Net fees and
commissions rose by 14% to $317 million, driven primarily by cash management and credit-related fees.
Additionally, fees from net trading income and foreign exchange gains increased slightly by 1%, to $191
million. Cards, trade services, and payment activities supported this growth. However, it was significantly
offset by a decline in fees from client-driven foreign currency sales due to regulatory-imposed FX liquidity
constraints in the UEMOA region. Furthermore, other income decreased by 11%, reaching $23 million.
Operating expenses increased by 13%, to $620 million in the first half of 2026 compared to the same period
last year. Staff costs rose by 16%, to $276 million, driven by continued investment in workforce capability
building through training and upskilling initiatives, as well as targeted investments in more sales and service
staff. Furthermore, other operating expenses increased by 12% to $304 million, primarily driven by higher
investments in technology. The cost-to-income ratio, which measures the efficiency of the company's
operations, improved to a record 48.4%, down from 49.1% in the same period last year. This improvement
was a result of net revenue growth outpacing the increase in operating expenses.
Pre-provision, pre-tax operating profit (PPOP), which is net revenue minus operating expenses, a key
metric for assessing the bank’s earnings power, increased by 16%, to $661 million, reflecting net revenue
momentum and prudent cost management.

Loans and advances to customers (gross) increased by $840 million year-on-year, representing a 7% rise
(7% in constant currency), to $12.4 billion as of 30 June 2026. In the UEMOA region, gross loans fell by 2%
(a 1% increase in constant currency), to $5.1 billion, with marginal growth driven by the CCB. In Nigeria,
loan balances decreased by 5% (a 4% decline in constant currency) to $1.5 billion. This reduction reflects
management's decision to reduce lending to address legacy asset quality issues while executing a capital
restoration plan that complies with regulatory requirements. In the AWA region, loans rose by 20% (26% in
constant currency), reaching $2.2 billion, driven by growth in the CCB business. Meanwhile, in the CESA
region, loans surged by 32% (29% in constant currency) to $2.8 billion, driven by expansion across all
business lines. By business line, CIB saw a 5% increase in loans, to $8.4 billion, primarily due to trade and
term loans. In contrast, CCB experienced a 12% increase in loans, reaching $4.1 billion, driven by growth in
SMEs, local corporates, and microlending.
Customer deposits increased by 13% (14% in constant currency) to $27.0 billion as of 30 June 2026. In the
UEMOA region, deposits grew by 11% (14% in constant currency) to $10.4 billion. In Nigeria, deposits rose
by 12% (1% in constant currency) to $2.6 billion. AWA achieved a remarkable 23% growth (29% in constant
currency) in deposits, to $6.5 billion, while CESA's deposits increased by 8% (in constant currency) to $7.3
billion. In terms of business lines, CIB saw its deposits grow by 18% to $11.2 billion, driven by strong
anchor relationships across client coverage, particularly with International Organisations. CCB deposits rose
by 10% to $15.8 billion, fueled by expanding primary banking relationships, enhanced product cross-selling,
and advancements in mobile money and digital banking capabilities. Overall, customer deposits remain
stable and diversified. The proportion of 'sticky' and low-cost CASA deposits as a percentage of total
customer deposits increased to 85.0% in the first half of 2026, up from 83.2% in the same period the previous
year. This improvement reflects ongoing efforts not only to grow deposits but also to enhance the deposit
mix and reduce the cost of customer deposits, which decreased to 1.8% in the current period from 1.9% in
the prior year. Consequently, the overall average cost of funding for the first half of 2026 was 2.3%, down
