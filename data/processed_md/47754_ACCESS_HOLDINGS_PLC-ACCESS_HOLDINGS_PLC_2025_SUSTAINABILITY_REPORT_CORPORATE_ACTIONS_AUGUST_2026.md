FOREWORD
At Access Holdings, we believe sustainability is not
simply about managing risks, it is about unlocking
opportunities, driving innovation, and creating long-
term value for our stakeholders and the communities
we serve.
As Africa continues to navigate economic, social, and
environmental transitions, we remain committed to
building a resilient institution that supports inclusive
growth while contributing meaningfully to sustainable
development. Sustainability considerations are
increasingly embedded across our governance,
strategy, risk management, and business operations,
strengthening our ability to respond to evolving
stakeholder expectations and emerging opportunities.
This report presents our sustainability-related and
climate-related disclosures for the year ended 31
December 2025, prepared in accordance with the
IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards (IFRS S1 and
IFRS S2), with complementary reference to the GRI
Standards and SASB framework.
The progress highlighted in this report reflects our
commitment to transparency, accountability, and
continuous improvement. While we are encouraged
by the strides made during the year, we recognise that
the journey towards a more sustainable future requires
ongoing collaboration, innovation, and disciplined
execution.
As we look ahead, we remain focused on creating shared
value for our shareholders, customers, employees,
communities, and the broader African economy,
while advancing our vision of becoming Africa’s most
respected and sustainable financial institution.

Framework
This report has been prepared in accordance with the IFRS Sus-
tainability Disclosure Standards issued by the International Sus-
tainability Standards Board (ISSB), specifically IFRS S1 General
Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial

as the Group’s primary sustainability reporting framework.
The Group’s sustainability reporting is supported by internal-
ly approved sustainability, risk management and governance
frameworks, policies and guidance documents, which provide the
foundation for the identification, assessment, management and
disclosure of sustainability-related and climate-related risks and
opportunities across the business.
In preparing these disclosures, the Group has applied IFRS S1 and
IFRS S2 as the primary reporting framework. Where no specific
IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standard directly applies to a sus-
tainability-related risk or opportunity, the Group has exercised
judgement in accordance with IFRS S1 and considered the appli-
cability of relevant SASB disclosure topics and metrics. The Group
has also referred to the GRI Standards only where such guidance
provides useful supplementary context and does not conflict with
the requirements of IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.
The Group has considered the applicability of disclosure topics
and metrics contained within the SASB Standards consistent with
the guidance provided in IFRS S1. The Group has further referred
to select GRI Standards, where relevant, to provide additional con-
text on sustainability impacts and stakeholder-related matters,
provided such references do not conflict with IFRS Sustainability
Disclosure Standards.

3.3 Climate Resilience and Scenario Analysis
In 2023, Access Bank partnered with the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and 1in1000 to conduct a pilot climate
transition risk assessment. This exercise leveraged the 1in1000 TRISK platform, an asset-level, bottom-up climate
stress testing tool, alongside the Paris Agreement Capital Transition Assessment (PACTA) framework to examine
how the Group’s corporate lending portfolio, specifically exposures in oil and gas, cement, and steel industries, would
perform under various climate transition pathways aligned with the Paris Agreement.
The assessment was conducted using two scenarios from the International Energy Agency’s (IEA) World Energy
Outlook (WEO):
Table 12: Climate Scenario Descriptions
Scenario Description
Stated Policies Scenario (STEPS) - Baseline Reflects the trajectory of the global energy system under the existing policy framework
and announced intentions. This scenario is not Paris-aligned, with projected warming
exceeding 2.5°C by century end.
Sustainable Development Scenario (SDS) - Shock Explores the energy outlook under significantly stricter climate policies, holding tem-
perature rise to just above 1.5°C by 2100, consistent with the Paris Agreement.
The results, which remained valid and applicable during the 2025 reporting period, indicated differentiated
sectoral impacts:
• Oil and Gas: Projections indicate mounting pressure, with production and profitability declining sharp-
ly from 2026 onward under the shock scenario, signaling the need for proactive portfolio recalibration.
• Power Generation: The Group’s renewable energy-focused power portfolio demonstrates robust
growth potential, effectively transforming transition risk into strategic opportunity.
These findings are actively informing the Group's capital allocation, risk appetite, and long-term strategy, supporting its
positioning for a low-carbon economy.
Drawing on these insights, the Group has identified clear priorities
to further strengthen its climate resilience framework. These
include completing physical climate risk modelling and fully
integrating it into its Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment
Process (ICAAP); and expanding the green loan portfolio,
which has grown from N22 billion in 2021 to N92.14 billion in
2025, toward a long-term target of N475 billion, with increased
financing for electric vehicles and other climate-friendly assets,
supported by N72.3 billion deployed under the Sustainable
Finance Framework during the reporting period. The Group is
also continuing to leverage development finance institution
partnerships, having mobilised $185.38 million (N266.83 billion) in
concessional DFI funding in 2025 alone, to improve sustainability.
